## Origin

Donald T. Campbell was a social psychologist who spent his career on how research designs fail. He stated the law in a 1976 paper on evaluating planned social change: the more any quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures and the more apt it will be to distort and corrupt the social processes it is intended to monitor. His running examples were crime statistics and standardized test scores.

## Mechanism

Campbell's claim is stronger than Goodhart's and is often collapsed into it. Goodhart says the measure stops being informative; Campbell says the thing being measured gets damaged. There are two routes. One is corruption of the measurement — reclassifying, excluding, reporting differently. The other is real: effort is redirected out of everything unmeasured and into the indicator, so the underlying process is genuinely reshaped rather than merely mis-reported.
