## Origin

Ian Betteridge, a British technology journalist, stated it in February 2009 while criticizing a TechCrunch article headlined with a question about whether Last.fm had handed listening data to the record industry. Andrew Marr had made the same observation in My Trade in 2004, describing the question-mark headline as a standard way of running a story you cannot stand up.

## Mechanism

A newsroom that had the evidence would write the assertion, because an assertion is a stronger headline and carries no risk when it is true. The question mark appears precisely when the reporting does not support the claim: it delivers the claim to the reader while leaving the outlet able to say it never made one. The construction is a marker of missing evidence, which is why it predicts the answer.
