## Origin

William of Ockham was a fourteenth-century English Franciscan and logician who used the principle constantly in the form plurality is not to be posited without necessity. He never wrote the sentence attached to his name; entities must not be multiplied beyond necessity comes from John Punch, an Irish scholastic, in 1639. The idea predates both of them — Aristotle and Ptolemy state versions of it.

## Mechanism

Every independent assumption is another thing that has to be true, and the probability of a conjunction falls as terms are added. A theory with more free parameters can also fit more possible observations, including the noise in the data you happen to have, so it is easier to fit and less informative when it does. Both effects appear formally in Bayesian model comparison, where extra parameters spread a model's predictions thinner and cost it probability mass.
