## Origin

Charles Goodhart was chief adviser at the Bank of England when he wrote the original in a 1975 paper for a Reserve Bank of Australia conference: any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes. He meant monetary aggregates — the relationships between money supply measures and inflation that had held while nobody was steering by them, and stopped holding once the Bank did. The compressed wording everyone quotes is Marilyn Strathern's, from a 1997 paper on British university audit.

## Mechanism

The correlation between a proxy and the thing you want was produced by a system that was not trying to move the proxy. Once it is a target, effort flows to whichever route raises the number most cheaply, and the cheapest route is almost never the one that also produces the goal. The measured quantity keeps moving; the relationship it was standing in for does not survive.
