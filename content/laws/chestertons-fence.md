## Origin

G. K. Chesterton put it in The Thing in 1929, in a chapter on why he would not join reformers who wanted institutions cleared away. He describes a fence across a road and a modern type who says he sees no use in it and proposes to clear it away. Chesterton's answer is procedural: go away and think until you can come back and tell me what it is for; then I may allow you to destroy it.

## Mechanism

A working safeguard hides the problem it was built for. The fence keeps the cattle off the road, so nobody alive has seen cattle on the road, so the fence looks unnecessary — the absence of the hazard is evidence the fence works, not evidence it is idle. Rules also outlive the people who wrote down the reason, which means the reason is usually unrecorded rather than nonexistent.
