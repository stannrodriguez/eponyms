## Origin

Theodore Sturgeon was a science fiction writer tired of critics who judged the genre by its worst paperbacks. He made the argument at a talk in the early 1950s and put it in print in his Venture Science Fiction book column in 1957–58, where he called it a revelation rather than a law: yes, ninety percent of science fiction is crud, and so is ninety percent of film, literature and consumer goods. What Sturgeon himself named Sturgeon's Law was a different line — nothing is always absolutely so.

## Mechanism

Quality is distributed with a long thin upper tail, so most of the mass of anything sits near the bottom. What survives from the past is the tail: the ninety percent has been discarded, out of print, demolished. Comparisons between an old field and a new one are therefore rigged — the old field is sampled at its tail and the new one in full.
