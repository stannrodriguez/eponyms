## Origin

In 2003 Barbra Streisand sued a photographer and his hosting service for fifty million dollars over an aerial photograph of the California coastline that included her Malibu house. The image was one of twelve thousand shot for an erosion survey and had been downloaded six times, two of those by her own lawyers. The month the suit became news it was seen well over four hundred thousand times. Mike Masnick named the effect on Techdirt in 2005.

## Mechanism

Suppression is a costly signal. Nobody spends money on a lawsuit over something unimportant, so the attempt is itself evidence that the material is worth seeing, and it converts a static file into a news event. The event is reportable in a way the original was not, and it cannot be suppressed by the same means — while copies spread faster than takedown notices can be served.

## Where it breaks

It is a survivorship claim. Suppression that works produces no story, so the successes are invisible by construction — non-disclosure agreements, quiet takedowns and pre-publication injunctions succeed routinely and unremarked. The effect needs a suppression attempt that is itself newsworthy, an audience that cares, and a copy that can outrun the notices. Take away any one of those and suppression simply works.

## Example

In 2011 a Premier League footballer obtained an anonymized injunction in the English courts barring publication of an alleged affair. His name was posted by tens of thousands of Twitter users within days, and a Member of Parliament then said it in the Commons, where privilege made the statement reportable. The injunction had been the only reason most of those people had heard of the story.
